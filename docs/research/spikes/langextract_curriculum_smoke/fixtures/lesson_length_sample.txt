Lesson 7: Comparing fractions with unlike denominators

Unit context: Students have used area models and number lines for equivalent fractions. This lesson connects tape diagrams to symbolic comparison.

Learning objectives
- Students will explain why a common denominator supports fair comparison of two fractions.
- Students will compare two fractions with different denominators using a strategy they can justify in writing.

Vocabulary: numerator, denominator, equivalent fraction, common denominator, benchmark

Opening / warm-up (10 minutes)
Display 3/4 and 5/8. Ask pairs to decide which is greater without calculating decimal values, then share one justification. Circulate and note whether students use benchmarks (e.g., one half), same-size parts, or visual sketches.

Main activity (25 minutes)
Students receive two problems: (1) compare 2/3 and 3/5 using a tape diagram; (2) compare 7/12 and 5/9 by finding a common denominator. Require a short written explanation for each strategy. For early finishers, extension: create a comparison that requires a denominator larger than 12.

Small-group support
Use fraction strips for students who struggle to partition tape diagrams evenly. Focus questions: "What does the whole represent?" and "Are the parts the same size in both fractions?"

Closure (5 minutes)
Exit ticket: Compare 4/6 and 3/4 in one sentence that names either a common denominator or a benchmark you used.

Materials: grid paper, fraction strips, activity handout problem set B.

Academic language sentence frame: "I know ___ is greater because ___ when the parts are the same size."

Note: Timing is approximate; adjust for your block schedule.
